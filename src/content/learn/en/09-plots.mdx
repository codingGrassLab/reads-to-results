## Plots before statistics: PCA

The first plot to make is not about any gene. It is about whether the samples behave as the design says they should. Transform the counts so that variance does not depend on the mean, then run a principal component analysis:

```r
vsd <- vst(dds, blind = FALSE)
plotPCA(vsd, intgroup = c("dex", "cell"))
```

<PCAPlot lang="en">PC1 ({pca.percentVar[0]}% of variance) separates dexamethasone from untreated; PC2 ({pca.percentVar[1]}%) separates donors. Colour: treatment; shape: cell line.</PCAPlot>

Read it as a check on the experiment:

- Treated and untreated samples split cleanly along PC1. The treatment effect is the biggest thing in the data. Good.
- Each cell line sits at a consistent PC2 height in both of its samples. The donor effect is real and large (24%), which is why `~ cell + dex` was the right design.
- No sample is off on its own. An outlier here is the first warning of a swapped sample, a failed library or a contamination.

`blind = FALSE` lets the transformation use the design to estimate dispersion, which is appropriate once you trust the design; use `blind = TRUE` the first time, when you are looking for problems.

A heatmap of sample-to-sample distances tells the same story in another form:

```r
library(pheatmap)
d <- dist(t(assay(vsd)))
pheatmap(as.matrix(d), clustering_distance_rows = d, clustering_distance_cols = d)
```

<DistanceMatrix lang="en">Euclidean distance between every pair of samples on the transformed data; dark is similar. Samples group first by treatment, then by donor.</DistanceMatrix>

## Volcano plot

After `results()`, the volcano plot shows every tested gene at once: effect size on the x axis, evidence on the y axis.

```r
library(EnhancedVolcano)
EnhancedVolcano(res, lab = res$symbol, x = "log2FoldChange", y = "padj",
                pCutoff = 0.05, FCcutoff = 1)
```

<VolcanoPlot lang="en">All {summary.genes_tested.toLocaleString("en-US")} tested genes. Red and blue: adjusted p below 0.05 and at least two-fold change ({summary.sig_005_lfc1} genes). Move the thresholds to see how the counts change.</VolcanoPlot>

The shape is typical and informative. The plot is roughly symmetric, so the drug turns genes off about as often as on. The strongest genes (SPARCL1, DUSP1, PER1, KLF15) are in the upper corners: large change *and* strong evidence. Genes far right but low down are large changes with poor evidence: low-count genes, exactly the ones shrinkage pulls in.

## MA plot

The MA plot puts the fold change against the mean expression, and so shows where in the expression range your changes are and whether low-count noise dominates:

```r
plotMA(res, ylim = c(-5, 5))      # unshrunken
plotMA(resLFC, ylim = c(-5, 5))   # apeglm-shrunken
```

<VolcanoPlot lang="en" mode="ma">Unshrunken, there is a fan of large fold changes at the left, where counts are low. Tick the box to switch to apeglm-shrunken fold changes: the fan collapses, and well-measured genes are untouched.</VolcanoPlot>

Coloured points pass both thresholds. The [MA plot page](../../read/ma-plot/) explains how to read it.

## Heatmap of top genes

A heatmap is for *patterns across samples*, not for showing significance. Take the most variable genes after transformation, centre each gene on its own mean, and cluster:

```r
topVar <- head(order(rowVars(assay(vsd)), decreasing = TRUE), 25)
mat <- assay(vsd)[topVar, ]
mat <- mat - rowMeans(mat)                      # centre each gene
ann <- as.data.frame(colData(vsd)[, c("dex", "cell")])
pheatmap(mat, annotation_col = ann, scale = "row")
```

<Heatmap lang="en">The 25 most variable genes. Rows are z-scores per gene, so colour means "above or below this gene's own average", not absolute expression.</Heatmap>

Two things to notice. The treated samples cluster together, as expected. And several of the most *variable* genes vary by **donor**, not by treatment: they form blocks that follow the cell-line annotation. "Most variable" is not "most differentially expressed". If you want the heatmap to show the treatment response, select genes by `padj` instead.

## Single genes

For the genes you will discuss in the text, show the actual counts:

```r
plotCounts(dds, gene = "ENSG00000103196", intgroup = c("dex", "cell"))
```

<GeneCounts lang="en">CRISPLD2: each line joins the untreated and treated sample of one donor. All four rise.</GeneCounts>

This is the plot that convinces a reviewer, because it shows the replicates. A fold change and a p-value hide whether the effect is consistent or driven by one sample.
