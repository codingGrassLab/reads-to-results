## Load the data

The `airway` package holds the full count matrix and the sample table as one object. Everything in this lesson starts from it (with your own Salmon output, `dds` comes from `DESeqDataSetFromTximport` as in lesson 7, and the rest is identical):

```r
library(airway); library(DESeq2)
data(airway)
se <- airway
se$dex <- relevel(se$dex, "untrt")     # make "untreated" the reference level
colData(se)[, c("cell", "dex")]
```

`relevel` matters. DESeq2 reports fold changes of the *other* levels against the reference, so with `untrt` as reference, a positive log2 fold change means "higher with dexamethasone". Without this line R uses alphabetical order, `trt` would be the reference, and every sign would be flipped.

## The design

```r
dds <- DESeqDataSet(se, design = ~ cell + dex)
```

`~ cell + dex` says: model each gene's expression as a baseline that depends on the cell line, plus an effect of dexamethasone. Because every cell line contributes one treated and one untreated sample, the `cell` term absorbs donor-to-donor differences, and the `dex` term is estimated *within* donors. This is the paired analysis from lesson 1. The variable you want to test goes **last** in the formula.

If you had, say, two groups of unrelated people, the design would simply be `~ condition`.

## Filter genes that cannot be tested

Of the {summary.genes_in_matrix.toLocaleString("en-US")} genes, most have almost no counts. They cannot show a difference, they slow things down, and they make multiple-testing correction harsher. Keep genes with at least 10 counts in at least 4 samples (the size of the smallest group):

```r
keep <- rowSums(counts(dds) >= 10) >= 4
dds <- dds[keep, ]
nrow(dds)
```

This is the standard filter from the DESeq2 vignette. Results are not sensitive to the exact threshold, but **filter before `DESeq()`**, not after looking at p-values.

## Run it

```r
dds <- DESeq(dds)
```

One function, three steps, each printed as it runs:

1. **estimating size factors**: one number per sample that corrects for sequencing depth and composition (the median-of-ratios method). The size factors here range from {Math.min(...Object.values(summary.size_factors)).toFixed(2)} to {Math.max(...Object.values(summary.size_factors)).toFixed(2)}; sample SRR1039513, with the smallest library, gets the smallest factor. The [normalization page](../../read/normalization/) shows what this does.
2. **estimating dispersions**: for each gene, how much biological variability there is beyond Poisson noise. With only four pairs, per-gene estimates are unreliable, so DESeq2 fits a trend across genes and shrinks each gene's estimate towards it. The [dispersion page](../../read/dispersion/) shows this.
3. **fitting the model and testing**: a negative binomial GLM per gene and a Wald test for the `dex` coefficient.

## The results table

```r
res <- results(dds, contrast = c("dex", "trt", "untrt"), alpha = 0.05)
summary(res)
```

`contrast = c("dex", "trt", "untrt")` states exactly what is compared; `alpha = 0.05` tells DESeq2 which adjusted-p threshold you will use, so that its independent filtering is tuned for it. The summary:

The columns:

- **baseMean**: mean of normalized counts across all samples. A gene with baseMean 5 and one with 5,000 can have the same fold change, but you trust the second much more.
- **log2FoldChange**: log2 of (treated / untreated). +1 means doubled, +2 means four times, −1 means halved. SPARCL1 at +{top[1].log2FoldChange.toFixed(1)} is up about {Math.round(2 ** top[1].log2FoldChange)}-fold. The [fold-change page](../../read/log2-fold-change/) goes deeper.
- **lfcSE**: the standard error of that estimate.
- **stat**: the Wald statistic, log2FoldChange divided by lfcSE.
- **pvalue**: the probability of a statistic this extreme if the gene did not change.
- **padj**: the p-value adjusted for testing {summary.genes_tested.toLocaleString("en-US")} genes at once (Benjamini–Hochberg). **This is the column to use.** With raw p below 0.05 you would call {summary.raw_p_005.toLocaleString("en-US")} genes, and roughly 5% of {summary.genes_tested.toLocaleString("en-US")}, about {Math.round(summary.genes_tested * 0.05)}, would be expected by chance alone. The [p-value page](../../read/pvalue-vs-padj/) shows why.

## Shrinking fold changes

Raw log2 fold changes for low-count genes are noisy: a gene with 3 counts against 30 shows "+3.3" but could easily be +1 or +5. For ranking genes and for plots, shrink the estimates toward zero in a way that leaves well-measured genes alone:

```r
library(apeglm)
resLFC <- lfcShrink(dds, coef = "dex_trt_vs_untrt", type = "apeglm")
```

For CRISPLD2 the shrunken value is {gene.result.shrunk_log2FoldChange.toFixed(2)} against {gene.result.log2FoldChange.toFixed(2)} unshrunk: almost no change, because the gene is well measured. For noisy genes the difference is large. Use **shrunken values for plots and ranking**, and report the unshrunken estimate with its confidence interval when you quote a single gene. The MA plot page shows the two side by side.

<div class="warn">
<strong>What NOT to do.</strong> (1) Do not run DESeq2 on normalized counts, TPM or FPKM; it needs the raw integers. (2) Do not filter by raw p-value. (3) Do not remove the <code>cell</code> term to "get more significant genes": without it, donor differences inflate the variance and you lose power, and the model is wrong for the design. (4) Do not test with <code>~ dex</code> on a subset of samples and compare with another subset; use contrasts within one model instead.
</div>
