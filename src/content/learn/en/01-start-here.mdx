## What RNA-seq measures

A cell makes thousands of different messenger RNAs at once. RNA-seq takes all of them, breaks them into short fragments, reads the sequence of each fragment, and then works out which gene each fragment came from. At the end you have, for every gene, a **count**: how many fragments were seen from that gene in that sample.

That is the whole measurement. Everything later, fold changes, p-values, pathways, is arithmetic on a table of counts.

Two things follow from this, and they shape every step of the analysis:

- **Counts are relative.** If you sequence one sample twice as deep, every gene gets about twice the count. So raw counts from two samples cannot be compared until you correct for how much was sequenced. That correction is called *normalization* (lesson 7).
- **Counts are noisy in a specific way.** Sampling fragments is like drawing balls from an urn: low counts are proportionally much noisier than high counts, and biological replicates add extra variability on top. Methods like DESeq2 exist because ordinary t-tests handle this badly.

## The experiment

We follow one real experiment all the way through. Himes and colleagues (2014) took **airway smooth muscle cells from four people** and, for each, treated one culture with the glucocorticoid **dexamethasone** for 18 hours and left another culture untreated. Glucocorticoids are the standard anti-inflammatory drugs for asthma, and the question was which genes they switch on and off in these cells.

Notice the design. Each donor contributes one treated and one untreated sample. This is a **paired design**: when we ask "what does dexamethasone do?", we can compare each treated sample with its own donor's untreated sample, and differences between donors cancel out. We will tell DESeq2 about this with the formula `~ cell + dex`.

<div class="note">
<strong>Why this dataset?</strong> It is small enough to run on a laptop, the signal is strong and biologically well understood (glucocorticoid responses are textbook material), and it ships with Bioconductor as the <code>airway</code> package, so anyone can reproduce every number on this site. It is also the dataset used in the official DESeq2 workflow, so what you learn here transfers directly to the documentation.
</div>
